# 08. Semaglutide + MRA/Finerenone Combination Evidence

**Clinical question:** Is semaglutide + finerenone an evidence-based combination?

## The data (`FLOW-MRA-2025`, Rossing, Diabetes Care 2025 — prespecified, non-randomized baseline-use subgroup; Figs. 1–2/Supplementary Tables 1–2; local source lines 75–219 and 239)

Baseline MRA use N=**257** (7.3% of the trial; 136 semaglutide/121 placebo) vs non-users N=3,276 (1,631/1,645). **Composition of the MRA subgroup:** spironolactone 218 (84.8%), eplerenone 38 (14.8%), esaxerenone 1 (0.4%), **finerenone 0 (0%)**. Finerenone was first approved (July 2021) after FLOW's recruitment window (June 2019–May 2021) had ended, so it is not represented at baseline at all.

| Outcome | MRA users | Non-users | P-interaction |
|---|---|---|---|
| FLOW primary five-component composite (including CV death) | HR 0.51 (0.30–0.86); 23/136 (16.9%) vs 36/121 (29.8%), 59 events | HR 0.79 (0.68–0.92); 308/1,631 (18.9%) vs 374/1,645 (22.7%), 682 events | 0.12 |
| Kidney-specific 4-component composite | HR 0.38 (0.15–0.84) | HR 0.82 (0.68–0.99) | 0.068 |
| RRT-initiation component | HR 0.18 (0.03–0.71), only 11 events | HR 0.91 (0.68–1.23) | 0.027 (uncorrected, few events) |
| 3-year absolute risk difference / **exploratory NNT** | −0.11 (−0.20, −0.01) / **NNT 9** | −0.04 (−0.07, −0.02) / **NNT 23** | — |
| UACR reduction, week 104 | 15% (−41, 31) | 33% (26, 39) | 0.22 |
| MACE / all-cause death | — | — | 0.75 / 0.89 |

New MRA initiation during the trial (post-randomization, non-randomized): 113 (6.4%) semaglutide vs 160 (9.1%) placebo initiated any MRA; among those, finerenone specifically was newly started in only 22 (1.2%) semaglutide vs 28 (1.6%) placebo — too few events to analyze on its own.

## Explicit inference boundaries

1. **This is a "semaglutide ± steroidal MRA (mainly spironolactone)" subgroup, not direct evidence for "semaglutide + finerenone."** Finerenone is nonsteroidal, with a different receptor-selectivity/tissue-distribution profile and its own dedicated hard-endpoint trial base (FIDELIO-DKD, FIGARO-DKD/FIDELITY) — intra-class interchangeability between steroidal and nonsteroidal MRAs cannot be assumed (CLAUDE.md rule 6).
2. **The numerically larger point estimate in MRA users (HR 0.51 vs 0.79) is not statistically significant (P-interaction=0.12) and is exploratory**, based on only 59 events among users. The subgroup RRT-component "significant" interaction (P=0.027) rests on only 11 events, is uncorrected for multiplicity, and should not be emphasized as a distinct finding.
3. **The published exploratory subgroup NNTs (9 vs 23) are not trial-level NNTs** and must not be presented as if they were; they are explicitly labeled exploratory by the source and rest on a small, non-randomized baseline subgroup.
4. **New on-trial MRA/finerenone initiation is a non-randomized, time-dependent post hoc analysis** ("hypothesis generating," per the source's own limitations), with only 50 total finerenone initiations across both arms — too few to analyze in isolation.
5. **Indirect evidence exists, in the opposite causal direction.** FIDELITY's own GLP-1RA-background subgroup (944 GLP-1RA users vs 12,082 non-users) found no significant interaction on finerenone's own treatment effect — i.e., does GLP-1RA background modify finerenone's effect, not the reverse question this section addresses. This is indirect, class-adjacent evidence; it was rechecked in the primary subgroup publication (`FIDELITY-GLP1-2023`; PMC10092103), but it still cannot answer whether adding semaglutide to finerenone is beneficial.
6. **No completed RCT has randomized semaglutide plus finerenone as a combination arm for hard-endpoint testing.** No such trial was located by any lane in this project.

## Real-world evidence and ongoing trials (explicitly lower than direct hard-outcome RCT evidence)

- `FIDELITY-GLP1-2023` randomized finerenone versus placebo, but **GLP-1RA background use was not randomized**: 944/13,026 participants used a GLP-1RA at baseline, event counts were small, CIs wide, and dose/type—including semaglutide specifically—could not be evaluated. This asks whether finerenone's effect is modified by GLP-1RA use, the reverse of adding semaglutide to finerenone. Locator: [primary PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10092103/), Results/Discussion; DOI 10.1111/dom.14883, PMID 36193847.
- `FIDELITY-TRIPLE-2026` analyzed only 167 participants on both SGLT2i and GLP-1RA at baseline. Finerenone improved surrogate trajectories across concomitant-treatment strata, but the subgroup was nonrandomized by background therapy and the most frequent combination was liraglutide+empagliflozin—not semaglutide-specific. Locator: [primary PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12860910/), Results/Supplementary Tables S1–S2; DOI 10.1016/j.ekir.2025.10.032, PMID 41630895.
- `FINERENONE-TRIPLE-RWE-2025` was an uncontrolled retrospective cohort of **51** people already using an SGLT2i plus one of liraglutide/dulaglutide/semaglutide before finerenone. At median 27 weeks, adjusted UACR changed −51.3%, while eGFR fell 3.92 mL/min/1.73m² and potassium rose 0.34 mmol/L. Molecule-specific outcomes and a counterfactual control were absent; this is surrogate/safety feasibility evidence, not additive efficacy. Locator: PubMed abstract Methods/Results; DOI 10.3390/jcm14228209, PMID 41303244.
- `RAPID-CKD-2026-REGISTRY` (NCT07547878) is a planned open-label phase 4 pilot randomizing **64** participants to rapid simultaneous versus usual sequential initiation of RASi, SGLT2i, finerenone, and semaglutide. At the 2026-09-05 registry check it remained **not yet recruiting** despite an estimated 2026-04-30 start; 6-month feasibility/UACR/eGFR/safety outcomes cannot establish hard-kidney efficacy. Locator: [ClinicalTrials.gov record/API](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07547878), study design/outcomes/status, first posted 2026-04-23.

Thus real-world and ongoing evidence makes co-administration plausible and testable, but does not close the core causal question. No result above proves semaglutide+finerenone—or four-drug therapy—reduces hard outcomes more than fewer-drug regimens.

## What can and cannot be claimed

**Supported:** No statistically significant treatment-by-baseline-MRA heterogeneity was detected for the FLOW primary five-component composite. Laboratory hyperkalemia >5.5 mEq/L was 22.1% vs 19.0% in MRA users and 13.6% vs 17.6% in non-users, P-interaction 0.12 (`FLOW-MRA-2025`, Safety; local lines 241–255). This small, non-randomized subgroup did not detect heterogeneity; it cannot establish absence of a clinically important interaction or the safety of semaglutide plus finerenone specifically.

**Not established:** A proven additive hard-kidney-outcome benefit of semaglutide **plus finerenone** specifically. Per CLAUDE.md rule 6, this project does not claim that semaglutide + finerenone has proven additive hard-kidney benefit.

## Current evidence for the combination is classified as follows

- **Direct randomized evidence:** none exists.
- **Indirect evidence:** FLOW's steroidal-MRA subgroup (above); FIDELITY's GLP-1RA-background subgroup (opposite direction of question); mechanistic complementarity (finerenone's anti-fibrotic/anti-inflammatory MRA pathway vs. semaglutide's metabolic/hemodynamic pathway, see `09`).
- **Observational/surrogate evidence:** the FIDELITY concomitant-treatment subgroups, a 51-person uncontrolled real-world series, and a not-yet-recruiting 64-person feasibility pilot above; none is semaglutide-specific hard-outcome confirmation.
- **Expert extrapolation / modeled evidence:** `COMBO-MODEL-NEUEN-2024` (Neuen, Circulation 2024, PubMed abstract; DOI 10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.123.067584, PMID 37952217) is an **actuarial lifetime-benefit simulation**, not a randomized trial and **not semaglutide-specific** — it models SGLT2i data from CANVAS/CREDENCE, finerenone data from FIDELIO-DKD/FIGARO-DKD, and a pooled GLP-1RA-CVOT estimate that does **not include FLOW**, applied to a single conventional-care population under an explicit assumption of full or 50% additivity of all three drug classes. Its modeled triple-combination MACE HR (0.65, 0.55–0.76), 3-year ARR (4.4%, 3.0–5.7), and NNT (23, 18–33) illustrate an optimistic **full-additivity scenario** under the model's assumptions; they are not a mathematical upper bound, observed randomized combination-therapy evidence, or “trial-proven.” The local adapted summary has unresolved derivative-rights metadata and is not a publication artifact.

## Preferred wording

> “FLOW's mineralocorticoid-receptor-antagonist subgroup analysis is dominated by spironolactone (finerenone was not available during FLOW's recruitment window and had zero baseline users). It shows a numerically larger point estimate for the FLOW primary five-component composite, including CV death, in MRA users than non-users, but no statistically significant heterogeneity was detected (P-interaction=0.12) in a small subgroup (59 events), and the exploratory subgroup NNTs (9 vs 23) should not be presented as trial-level NNTs. No randomized trial evidence establishes an additive hard-kidney-outcome benefit of semaglutide plus finerenone specifically; modeled lifetime-benefit estimates for triple therapy are indirect extrapolations under an assumed-additive-effects framework.”
