# 11. Guideline and Regulatory Evolution

All statuses verified as of the 2026-09-05 cutoff.

## Regulatory status by jurisdiction — official distinctions preserved

| Jurisdiction | Source ID | Status | Indication structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| United States | `FDA-OZEMPIC-APPROVAL-S025-2025` (approval chronology) + `FDA-OZEMPIC-USPI-S038-2026` (current wording) | Initial CKD-risk-reduction approval **2025-01-28** (supplement S-025); current label is supplement S-038 / DailyMed revised **2026-05** | **Standalone third indication bullet**: "to reduce the risk of sustained eGFR decline, end-stage kidney disease, and cardiovascular death in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic kidney disease" |
| European Union | `EMA-OZEMPIC-SMPC-2026` | FLOW data incorporated via a December 2024 CHMP positive opinion | Section 4.1 (Therapeutic indications) remains the **standard T2D glycaemic-control indication**, directing readers to sections 4.4/4.5/5.1 for kidney-trial data; the FLOW-trial description itself sits in **Section 5.1 (Pharmacodynamic properties)**. **The EU has NOT granted an independent CKD risk-reduction indication** analogous to the US label. |
| Taiwan | `TFDA-OZEMPIC-2026` | Label revision date **2026-01-26** (a revision date, not a confirmed first-approval date) | **Standalone third indication bullet** (short English paraphrase of the official Chinese text, not a verbatim Chinese quote): reduction of sustained eGFR decline, progression to end-stage kidney disease, or cardiovascular death in people with T2D and existing CKD — parallels the FDA structure |

**Do not describe the three jurisdictions as having "substantively identical" or "aligned" indication wording.** FDA and Taiwan TFDA both list an independent CKD indication; the EU incorporates the same trial evidence only into the efficacy/pharmacodynamics section of a glycaemic-control label. This is a difference in regulatory presentation of the same underlying trial evidence, not a difference in the evidence itself.

**Three distinctions that must not be collapsed** (master prompt §XX):
1. **Regulatory indication ≠ FLOW trial enrollment criteria.** FLOW's enrolled population was eGFR 25–75 with UACR-dependent pathways (`02`); the indication text ("adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic kidney disease") is literally broader than the trial's precise enrolled population.
2. **Regulatory listing ≠ guideline sequencing recommendation.** No regulatory text specifies whether semaglutide should be used before, after, or simultaneously with RASi/SGLT2i/finerenone.
3. **Regulatory indication ≠ proof of combination-therapy additive benefit.** Approval reflects FLOW's overall-population effect (background RASi near-universal, SGLT2i 15.6%, MRA 7.3% with zero finerenone) — it does not represent that any regulator has evaluated or endorsed additive benefit from combining semaglutide with SGLT2i or finerenone specifically (see `07`, `08`).

## Guideline evolution — before and after FLOW

### What changed

| Era/document | Directly verified position | Interpretation for semaglutide and CKD |
|---|---|---|
| `KDIGO-DIABETES-CKD-2022` — [KDIGO 2022 Diabetes in CKD](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/KDIGO-2022-Diabetes-Guideline.pdf) (final) | Figure 23/Practice Points 4.1–4.3 put lifestyle plus **metformin and an SGLT2i** in first-line glycemic therapy. Recommendation 4.2.1 recommends a long-acting GLP-1RA when individualized glycemic targets remain unmet despite metformin/SGLT2i, or when those agents cannot be used (1B); Practice Point 4.2.1 prioritizes agents with documented CV benefit (PDF pp.24–27). FLOW was still an ongoing trial in this guideline. | Predominantly an **additional glucose-lowering/CV-benefit** position after metformin/SGLT2i, not a dedicated kidney-outcome role. Its advanced-CKD/dialysis algorithm lists feasible drug choices but does not constitute randomized semaglutide outcome efficacy in dialysis. |
| `KDIGO-CKD-2024` — [KDIGO 2024 CKD Guideline](https://kdigo.org/guidelines/ckd-evaluation-and-management/kdigo-2024-ckd-guideline/) (final; published 2024-03-13) | CKD-wide guideline reinforced eGFR/UACR risk assessment and broad SGLT2i disease-modifying therapy; it was completed/published before the July 2024 FLOW primary paper. | It supplies kidney-first background and keeps SGLT2i foundational, but **cannot be read as a post-FLOW adjudication of semaglutide**. Silence on FLOW is chronology, not a negative recommendation. |
| [`ADA-STANDARDS-2025`](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/48/Supplement_1/S239/157554/11-Chronic-Kidney-Disease-and-Risk-Management) (final; DOI 10.2337/dc25-S011) | Recommendation 11.5a retained an SGLT2i with demonstrated benefit for T2D+CKD at eGFR≥20 (A). Recommendation 11.5b recommended a GLP-1 agonist with demonstrated benefit in this population to reduce CV risk **and kidney disease progression** (A); the accompanying CKD treatment discussion identified semaglutide as another first-line agent after FLOW. | First ADA annual Standards after FLOW shifted GLP-1RA from mainly CV/secondary-kidney context to an explicit kidney-progression recommendation. This does not make SGLT2i dispensable or prove combination additivity. |
| `ADA-STANDARDS-2026` (final) | Chapter 11 Recommendation 11.7a keeps SGLT2i kidney/CV protection foundational (initiate at eGFR≥20; may continue until kidney failure, A); 11.7b recommends a GLP-1 agonist with demonstrated benefit to reduce kidney progression and CV risk in T2D+CKD (A). Chapter 9 Recommendations 9.10–9.11 add phenotype-specific glycemic/advanced-CKD language (below). | Consolidates semaglutide's organ-protection role while preserving an SGLT2-first kidney/HF evidence axis. It also introduces lower-certainty dialysis language that must be separated from randomized outcome proof. |
| `CKM-GUIDELINE-2026` (final, 2026-06-09) | Official AHA summary uses eGFR+UACR to guide care; RASi and SGLT2i are first-line for CKD+T2D or CKD+albuminuria; with persistent albuminuria in CKD+T2D, add an nsMRA **or** GLP-1–based therapy. Agent selection should follow CKD, ASCVD, HF, obesity, severe hyperglycemia, and MASLD. | Moves beyond a glucose-only ladder to phenotype- and absolute-risk-based CKM layering. “Or” does not establish equivalence or a semaglutide-versus-finerenone winner; no head-to-head or sequencing RCT exists. |
| `KDIGO-DIABETES-CKD-UPDATE-2026-DRAFT` | Official KDIGO site still identified this as a March 2026 **public-review draft** at the 2026-09-05 cutoff; comments closed 2026-04-13 and no final was identified. | Potential post-FLOW kidney-guideline change remains pending. Draft wording must not be represented as current finalized KDIGO policy. |

### ADA 2026: the exact clinical boundaries

The final official [Chapter 11](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S246/163914/11-Chronic-Kidney-Disease-and-Risk-Management) and [Chapter 9](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S183/163934/9-Pharmacologic-Approaches-to-Glycemic-Treatment) pages were directly checked in the repair audit, superseding the earlier lane note that recommendation numbers were provisional:

- **11.7a (A):** SGLT2i with demonstrated benefit for T2D+CKD to reduce CKD progression/CV events; initiate at eGFR≥20 and continuation is allowed until kidney failure.
- **11.7b (A):** GLP-1 agonist with demonstrated benefit in this population to reduce kidney disease progression and CV risk. This is not conditioned on failure to reach an HbA1c target.
- **9.10 (A):** for T2D with confirmed eGFR 20–60 and/or albuminuria, use an SGLT2i or GLP-1RA with demonstrated benefit for glycemic management, CKD slowing, and CV-event reduction **irrespective of A1c**. It does not say the two classes have identical kidney-failure evidence.
- **9.11 (B/C):** at eGFR<30, GLP-1RA is preferred for glycemic management because of lower hypoglycemia risk and CV-event evidence (B). The additional statement that a non-renally cleared GLP-1–based therapy may be initiated/continued on dialysis to reduce CV risk/mortality is grade C; Chapter 11 repeats this as 11.11b. As detailed in `10`, no randomized maintenance-dialysis efficacy trial establishes that outcome benefit, so the grade-C guideline judgment and the evidence gap must be shown together.
- **11.9 (B):** simultaneous SGLT2i+finerenone initiation may be considered at UACR≥100 mg/g, eGFR 30–90, on RASi, based on safety/albuminuria evidence. This is **not** direct proof of hard-outcome additivity, and its UACR≥100 threshold must not be conflated with the separately reported, still-unverified KDIGO-draft/“derived from FLOW” rationale.

### KDIGO 2026 draft: status before content

The [official KDIGO topic page](https://kdigo.org/guidelines/diabetes-ckd/) and [public-review announcement](https://kdigo.org/kdigo-2026-diabetes-and-ckd-guideline-draft-available-for-public-review/) establish status only: **draft, not final**. Direct extraction of the draft PDF failed in the project. Secondary summaries report movement toward “foundational” RASi/SGLT2i/statin plus risk-based nsMRA/GLP-1RA therapy, a possible nsMRA recommendation, and a possible UACR≥100 mg/g rationale linked to FLOW. None of those operative details was verified against the draft itself, and none may be quoted as settled policy. The unresolved primary-document check is carried in `01` rather than hidden behind a PASS label.

### CKM 2026: sequencing and phenotype priorities

The official [AHA “Top Things to Know”](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2026-guideline-for-the-prevention-detection-evaluation-and-management-of-ckm-syndrome/top-things-to-know) provides usable official summary text even though the restricted cached author-manuscript cannot be redistributed. Its operational message is not a rigid four-step ladder:

| Dominant clinical problem | Defensible priority/readout | What remains unproved |
|---|---|---|
| CKD with T2D or albuminuria; especially HF risk | RASi (when indicated/tolerated) plus SGLT2i are first-line kidney/HF-protective therapy. | FLOW does not displace this foundation; no trial ranks semaglutide against an SGLT2i. |
| Persistent albuminuria despite foundation | Add nsMRA **or** GLP-1–based therapy for further kidney/CV protection; selection must consider potassium/eGFR, GI/volume/nutrition risk, CV phenotype, and patient priorities. | No head-to-head semaglutide-vs-finerenone trial and no randomized full-stack sequencing study. Finerenone has dedicated diabetic-CKD trials; semaglutide has FLOW plus weight/glycemic/CV effects. Different strengths do not establish superiority. |
| Obesity and/or established ASCVD | GLP-1–based therapy becomes especially attractive for multidomain weight, glycemic, ASCVD, and—when FLOW-like—kidney benefit. | SELECT's 2.4-mg/non-diabetic population and FLOW's 1.0-mg T2D+CKD population cannot be collapsed into a dose-independent claim. |
| HFmrEF/HFpEF | AHA summary places SGLT2i first-line; add GLP-1–based therapy when obesity or other CKM risks are present; consider nsMRA for T2D+CKD. | Semaglutide is not a substitute for HF guideline-directed therapy. |
| Severe hyperglycemia | Severe hyperglycemia is an explicit agent-selection axis; a GLP-1RA may meet several long-term goals when clinically suitable. | The CKM list is not a mandate that semaglutide always precede insulin or urgent stabilization; exact treatment depends on the clinical presentation. |
| MASLD plus overweight/obesity | MASLD is an explicit CKM selection axis; ADA 2026 Recommendation 9.12 supports considering a GLP-1RA with demonstrated MASH benefit in T2D+MASLD+overweight/obesity. | This is a liver/metabolic indication axis, not evidence of additional kidney-effect magnitude. |
| Advanced CKD/dialysis/frailty | Separate glucose control, CV-risk reasoning, and tolerability from kidney-outcome efficacy; prioritize hypoglycemia avoidance and nutrition/volume surveillance. | Dialysis initiation/continuation language is lower-certainty; randomized CV, weight, glycemic, and kidney efficacy remain incompletely established (`10`). |

PREVENT-estimated 10-/30-year CVD risk helps CKM staging and treatment prioritization, but it is not a kidney-failure risk equation and does not supply a semaglutide-specific absolute kidney benefit (`CKM-GUIDELINE-2026`, official JACC guideline, §4.1 “Quantitative Assessment of CVD Risk”). The official AHA/JACC web materials reviewed for this project did not independently verify the complete evidence-search date range found in the restricted cached manuscript, so those dates are not reproduced here. Do not assume that any individual later publication was incorporated: compare its publication date with the guideline and consult the guideline's own evidence tables before making that claim.

## "Four pillars" — origin and status (explicit adjudication)

The phrase is **not** used in `FLOW-PRIMARY-2024` itself, which instead frames semaglutide as a fourth option to consider alongside three existing "guideline-directed medical therapies" (RAS inhibition, SGLT2 inhibition, mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonism with finerenone), while explicitly noting the trial "was not powered to detect differences within and between important subgroups" for combination assessment. The phrase **"fourth pillar"/"four pillars"** is explicitly used by `FLOW-MRA-2025` (Rossing, a FLOW-trial-affiliated author paper), citing its own prior reference as the concept's origin. **No official KDIGO, ADA, or CKM guideline has been confirmed by this project to use "four pillars" as a formal, operationalized recommendation title or term** — the KDIGO 2026 draft may be moving toward a semantically similar framework ("foundational + additional risk-based therapy"), but its draft status and unverified verbatim content mean this must remain a flagged uncertainty, not an established fact. **Conclusion:** "four pillars" should be characterized in the final synthesis as trial-affiliated-author narrative/advocacy terminology, useful conceptually, not as guideline-endorsed nomenclature, until a finalized guideline is confirmed to use it verbatim.

## Guideline-vs-trial timing caveats

The August 2026 SELECT/FLOW/SOUL pooled analysis necessarily postdates the June 2026 CKM guideline. For other 2026 FLOW subgroup/dialysis papers, this project does not infer inclusion or exclusion merely from calendar proximity; consult the guideline evidence tables before attributing consideration of a specific paper.
