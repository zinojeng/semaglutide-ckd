# 13. Clinical Decision Framework

This is an evidence-organization synthesis, not a verbatim restatement of any single official guideline, and not individualized patient advice (CLAUDE.md Safety section). Grading uses the calibrated scale: established / strongly supported / suggestive / hypothesis-generating / unknown.

## Foundational principles

1. **RASi at maximally tolerated labeled dose is foundational** for all FLOW-eligible-phenotype scenarios (decades of dedicated RCT evidence outside this project's own re-verification scope; ~95% background use in FLOW itself). RASi is not unconditionally indicated in every CKD phenotype — individualize per contraindications (e.g., hyperkalemia risk).
2. **SGLT2 inhibitors** have the broadest dedicated hard-kidney-outcome RCT base spanning a wider eGFR range than FLOW (CREDENCE, DAPA-CKD, EMPA-KIDNEY — contextual only, `12`), including non-diabetic CKD. They remain foundational kidney/HF-protective therapy per the closed Wave-2 CKM-lane position and ADA 2026 framing (11.7a).
3. **Finerenone/nsMRA** has dedicated hard-endpoint evidence in albuminuric diabetic CKD (FIDELIO-DKD/FIGARO-DKD, contextual only) for persistent albuminuria despite RASi±SGLT2i.
4. **Semaglutide (via FLOW)** has established randomized evidence for the five-component composite, MACE, and all-cause mortality in the specific FLOW-eligible T2D+albuminuric-CKD phenotype (eGFR 25–75 by pathway, `02`). Its distinctive contribution relative to the other three pillars is its combined kidney/CV/mortality/glycemic/weight profile within a single trial, not demonstrated *superiority* in kidney-failure prevention specifically (`03`).
5. **No completed RCT randomizes the sequencing or full combination of these four classes against each other.** Any stacking order below is a suggestive-to-hypothesis-generating default operational skeleton, not a mandatory flowchart, and is explicitly scoped to the FLOW-like T2D+albuminuric-CKD phenotype — it should not be applied to a predominantly obesity/ASCVD-without-albuminuric-CKD phenotype (see Patient E below and `06`'s SELECT discussion).

## Suggested default operational sequence (FLOW-like phenotype only; suggestive, not mandatory)

1. Confirm/optimize RASi to maximally tolerated dose.
2. Add an SGLT2 inhibitor once RASi is stable and eGFR meets the drug's labeled lower bound.
3. If albuminuria persists and potassium/eGFR permit, add finerenone/nsMRA (monitor potassium).
4. Consider adding semaglutide **at the FLOW-validated dose and route (SC 1.0 mg weekly)** — do not extrapolate FLOW's kidney evidence to SELECT's 2.4 mg SC or SOUL's 14 mg oral dose/route. Trigger conditions can include glycemic non-attainment, overweight/obesity, ASCVD/HF comorbidity, or persistent kidney risk. **Glycemic attainment is not a gate**: current FDA/Taiwan labeling carries no HbA1c-target qualifier for the CKD indication (established, label-text fact), while FLOW's own HbA1c-subgroup consistency is only suggestive support (not a formal interaction test) — do not derive the "no HbA1c gate" recommendation from FLOW's HbA1c enrollment ceiling (≤10%) alone.

This order is not a mandatory flowchart. In high-risk phenotypes, simultaneous or near-simultaneous initiation of multiple classes may fit an “early intervention accumulates longer event-free time” logic — this is illustrated only by `COMBO-MODEL-NEUEN-2024`'s modeled (not randomized, not semaglutide-specific) lifetime simulation and its optimistic full-additivity scenario, not by trial-proven combination data or a mathematical upper bound. Neither higher KDIGO risk nor higher UACR has a published FLOW stratum-specific ARR/NNT.

## Endocrinology and nephrology lenses — complementary, not competing

| Decision dimension | Endocrinology lens | Nephrology lens | Joint, evidence-bounded interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline endpoint | Values one agent's glycemic, weight, MACE, mortality, and kidney-composite profile | First asks whether the “kidney” endpoint includes CV death and whether kidney failure itself changed | Lead with the FLOW five-component composite **including CV death** (HR 0.76), then report the supportive kidney-specific four-component composite (HR 0.79) and the individually nonsignificant KRT/eGFR<15/kidney-death components (`03`). |
| eGFR and UACR | May see two additional organ-risk markers improved alongside HbA1c/weight | Separates acute from chronic slope, checks creatinine artifact, and treats UACR as a surrogate unless mediation is shown | FLOW had no semaglutide-specific differential dip through week 12 (an earlier resolved transient is not excluded) and a +0.94 chronic-slope difference; creatinine/cystatin-C near-concordance lowers concern for a pure creatinine-generation artifact, but the difference CI was not reported and weight independence remains unknown. UACR fell, but whether it mediates the hard outcome remains unknown (`03`, `09`). |
| SGLT2i priority | May prioritize semaglutide earlier when obesity, glycemic need, or ASCVD dominates | Usually prioritizes SGLT2i for the broadest dedicated kidney/HF evidence, including non-diabetic CKD | Phenotype governs order. FLOW does not prove semaglutide additivity on SGLT2i (baseline-user HRs 1.07/1.18 with wide CIs); do not force a universal sequence (`07`, `11`). |
| HbA1c and weight | These are therapeutic benefits and may justify treatment even before kidney evidence is considered | Avoids mistaking HbA1c/weight improvement for proof of renal mediation; watches nutritional reserve | US/Taiwan CKD labeling has no HbA1c-target gate, but mechanistic independence from HbA1c/weight is not proven. Separate organ-protection indication from glycemic escalation (`09`, `11`). |
| Advanced CKD and kidney failure | Low intrinsic hypoglycemia risk and retained pharmacokinetics are attractive | Requires an explicit eGFR/UACR eligibility check and distinguishes “no dose adjustment” from efficacy below eGFR 25 or on dialysis | eGFR<25 and dialysis lack randomized efficacy evidence; post-dialysis FLOW data are continuation-safety descriptions only (`10`, `12`). |
| Volume depletion and sarcopenia | Slow titration and GI management preserve adherence | GI losses plus diuretics/SGLT2i can cause prerenal AKI; weight loss may hide lean-mass loss in frail CKD | Use sick-day instructions, renal-function/volume monitoring, and nutrition/functional surveillance; FLOW did not measure frailty or sarcopenia, so absence of a signal is not evidence of absence (`10`, `12`). |

## Monitoring considerations under combination therapy

| Risk | Relevant combination | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Applicability lower bound (exclusion boundary, not a monitoring issue) | Any combination in a patient with eGFR<25 or on dialysis | Outside FLOW's randomized efficacy evidence entirely (`10`, `12`) — cannot be "monitored around"; requires individualized judgment and, where relevant, the descriptive dialysis-safety data only |
| Hyperkalemia | RASi + finerenone (± steroidal MRA) | Baseline/periodic potassium and eGFR monitoring; FLOW's small steroidal-MRA subgroup did not detect treatment heterogeneity for laboratory hyperkalemia (P-interaction 0.12), but cannot establish safety with finerenone (`08`) |
| Volume depletion / prerenal AKI | SGLT2i + semaglutide, especially with GI symptoms, diuretics, elderly/frail patients | Sick-day-rules education; in overall FLOW serious-AE preferred-term counts, acute kidney injury showed no numerical excess (124 vs 123). No retrieved source established AKI safety by KDIGO stratum (`10`). |
| GI adverse-event stacking | Semaglutide's own GI burden | AE-driven discontinuation 13.2% vs 11.9%, GI-specific 4.5% vs 1.1% (`10`) |
| Creatinine-based eGFR misinterpretation | SGLT2i's acute dip; semaglutide's weight/possible lean-mass reduction | FLOW's near-identical creatinine- and cystatin-C-based eGFR benefit argues against a pure muscle-loss artifact (`09`), but is a single-trial, industry-sponsored concordance check, not independent confirmation; lean-mass/sarcopenia outcome data remain a genuine gap (`10`, `12`) |
| Hypoglycemia | GLP-1RA + insulin/sulfonylurea, especially advanced CKD | Standard practice: reduce insulin/SU dose at initiation, individualize |

## Five patient scenarios (evidence-graded)

### Patient A — T2D + CKD, UACR 800, eGFR 42, obesity

FLOW-eligible (eGFR 25–<50 pathway; UACR well above the 100 mg/g threshold and near FLOW's own median of 567.6). **Grade: established** for kidney/CV/mortality benefit at the FLOW dose/population match. Obesity adds a genuine multidomain rationale, but the FLOW-validated dose (1.0 mg SC) should not be assumed to deliver SELECT-level (2.4 mg) weight loss — dose escalation for weight-management purposes is a separate decision without dedicated-CKD-trial safety/efficacy data at 2.4 mg. Two separate publications/analyses of the same FLOW cohort reported exploratory UACR–mortality interactions (UACR<300-vs-≥300, P=.01; UACR≥2,000 subgroup HR 0.47, P-interaction=.02). They are not independent replications and remain **exploratory/hypothesis-generating only**, not a basis for differential counseling or an inferred larger absolute benefit.

### Patient B — T2D + CKD, UACR 150, eGFR 35, already on ACEi + SGLT2i

FLOW-eligible by pathway (eGFR 25–<50, UACR 150>100), though at a lower UACR than FLOW's typical enrolled phenotype. Maps onto FLOW's underpowered baseline-SGLT2i subgroup: FLOW primary five-component composite including CV death HR 1.07 (0.69–1.67); kidney-specific composite HR 1.18 (0.71–1.98); P-interaction 0.109/0.100 (`07`). **Grade: unknown**, not suggestive, for incremental hard-kidney benefit specifically from adding semaglutide on top of SGLT2i — wide intervals are compatible with benefit, no effect, or harm. Any decision to add semaglutide here should be justified on its overall-population evidence and the patient's glycemic, weight, CV, and mortality priorities rather than presented as proven additive hard-kidney efficacy.

### Patient C — T2D, eGFR 25, poor glycemic control

At FLOW's absolute lower eGFR boundary, **UACR is required before claiming protocol-matched FLOW applicability**. If UACR is **>100 and <5,000 mg/g**, the patient meets pathway B (subject to all other criteria); if screening UACR is **≤100 mg/g**, the patient is outside FLOW eligibility. A baseline-reclassified UACR<100 subgroup nevertheless appeared after enrollment (n=350; 13/177 vs 17/173 events; HR 0.70, 0.34–1.44), but the wide CI and screening-to-baseline reclassification do not establish efficacy in persistent low-UACR/normoalbuminuric CKD. Among participants with eGFR<30, the FLOW five-component composite including CV death had HR 0.81 (0.58–1.13): directionally favorable but imprecise, not a kidney-specific result. Poor glycemic control is a separate therapeutic indication. **Grade: strongly supported** for glycemic treatment; **suggestive** for the FLOW composite only if UACR satisfies the enrollment pathway, and **unknown** for protocol-matched FLOW efficacy if UACR≤100. There is no randomized FLOW evidence below eGFR 25 (`02`, `04`, `12`). Conservative titration and close volume/renal monitoring are especially important given lower physiologic reserve (`10`).

### Patient D — T2D + CKD, persistent albuminuria despite ACEi + SGLT2i + finerenone

The quadruple-therapy question, which the master prompt explicitly forbids overreading. FLOW's own MRA subgroup (7.3% of the trial) is overwhelmingly steroidal (spironolactone/eplerenone), with zero baseline finerenone users (`08`). **Grade: not established** — per CLAUDE.md rule 6, this project does not claim proven additive hard-kidney benefit of semaglutide on top of ACEi+SGLT2i+finerenone. `COMBO-MODEL-NEUEN-2024` (Neuen 2024, Circulation) — an independent actuarial lifetime-benefit simulation that does not include FLOW or semaglutide-specific trial data as an input — offers a mechanistically complementary rationale and an optimistic *modeled* (not randomized) full-additivity projection. This should be presented to the patient as hypothesis-generating/model-assumption evidence only, never as a mathematical upper bound or demonstrated outcome data for this specific quadruple combination.

### Patient E — T2D + CKD + obesity + established ASCVD

The phenotype where the evidence base converges most completely: FLOW-level kidney/CV/mortality evidence (if FLOW-eligible by eGFR/UACR), SOUL-level ASCVD MACE reduction in T2D, and SELECT-adjacent (non-diabetic) obesity/ASCVD weight-and-CV evidence all point the same direction. **Grade: established** for kidney/CV/mortality benefit if FLOW-eligible; **strongly supported** for the general proposition that a single multidomain agent is an efficient choice across glycemic, weight, ASCVD, and kidney risk simultaneously. **Explicit caveat:** no single trial tested this exact four-domain combined phenotype end-to-end at one dose — this is a cross-trial synthesis across different doses/populations (FLOW 1.0 mg SC / SELECT 2.4 mg SC / SOUL 14 mg oral), not a single RCT's endpoint, and should be labeled as such. **This patient's dominant phenotype determines scope:** if the primary concern is obesity/ASCVD rather than albuminuric CKD (a SELECT-type phenotype), the RASi→SGLT2i→finerenone CKD-oriented ladder above does not apply as written; dose/formulation selection should follow the phenotype-appropriate trial evidence (SELECT's 2.4 mg for weight/ASCVD-dominant presentations) rather than FLOW's 1.0 mg kidney-outcomes dose.

## Absolute-benefit note (applies across all five scenarios)

The only published phenotype-specific absolute estimates in this evidence base are the descriptive 3-year NNTs for the FLOW five-component composite in three **CV phenotypes**: heart failure 13, high-risk without established CVD 17, and ASCVD 22 (`FLOW-CVPHENOTYPE-2026`, structured abstract/results; DOI 10.1016/j.jacc.2026.02.5125, PMID 42233552). No eGFR-, UACR-, or KDIGO-stratum ARR/NNT is published, and no kidney-specific-composite subgroup NNT is available. Therefore the globally “greatest absolute-benefit phenotype” is unknown; do not extrapolate the three CV-phenotype NNTs to CKD severity (`04`, `12`).
