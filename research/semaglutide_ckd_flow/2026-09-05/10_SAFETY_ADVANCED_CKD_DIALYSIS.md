# 10. Safety in CKD, Advanced CKD, and Dialysis

All FLOW figures are from `FLOW-PRIMARY-2024`, Table 3, journal p.120 (local PDF p.12), and/or `FLOW-SUPPLEMENT-2024`, Table S4, supplement pp.28–31, or Table S5, supplement p.32, as identified below (N=1,767 semaglutide / N=1,766 placebo).

## Gastrointestinal adverse events and discontinuation

- GI-disorder serious adverse events: 5.4% vs 5.3% (not different).
- **Adverse-event-driven permanent discontinuation:** 233 (13.2%) semaglutide vs 211 (11.9%) placebo.
- **GI-specific permanent discontinuation (a subset of the above):** 79 (4.5%) vs 20 (1.1%) — nausea 23 (1.3%) vs 5 (0.3%); diarrhea 15 (0.9%) vs 2 (0.1%); vomiting 6 (0.3%) vs 2 (0.1%).
- **Unresolved, separate discrepancy (do not conflate with the above two figures):** overall any-reason permanent discontinuation is reported as **26%** (`FLOW-PRIMARY-2024`, pooled both arms) vs **28.8%** (`FLOW-SGLT2-2024`/Mann, pooled both arms). Both papers cite identical companion statistics (89% adherence, 98.6% vital-status-confirmed), arguing against a different population or analysis window; no source checked in this project establishes the reason for the gap. This is not decision-critical for efficacy but must not be silently harmonized.

Locator: GI-disorder serious AEs are in `FLOW-SUPPLEMENT-2024`, Table S4, “Gastrointestinal disorders,” supplement p.29; overall AE-driven discontinuation and the GI/nausea/diarrhea/vomiting subsets are in `FLOW-PRIMARY-2024`, Table 3, journal p.120 (local PDF p.12), and `FLOW-SUPPLEMENT-2024`, Table S5, supplement p.32 (local lines 951–965). The 26% value is `FLOW-PRIMARY-2024`, Results—“Trial Participants,” journal p.113; 28.8% is `FLOW-SGLT2-2024`, Results—“Participants and baseline characteristics” (local line 106). The discrepancy is mapped in `12` §A.

## Volume depletion / acute kidney injury — prerenal, not direct nephrotoxicity

FLOW shows no numerical excess in the following **serious-adverse-event preferred terms** (not all renal events): acute kidney injury 124 (7.0%) vs 123 (7.0%); CKD 42 (2.4%) vs 37 (2.1%); end-stage renal disease 31 (1.8%) vs 36 (2.0%); renal failure 8 (0.5%) vs 17 (1.0%); dehydration 10 (0.6%) vs 10 (0.6%). The current US label (`FDA-OZEMPIC-USPI-S038-2026`) carries a postmarketing warning for AKI (sometimes requiring hemodialysis), occurring mostly in patients with GI reactions leading to dehydration, and instructs renal-function monitoring during initiation/dose escalation. This supports a **prerenal, volume-depletion-mediated** pathway, not evidence of direct nephrotoxicity, and is consistent with FLOW's absence of a numerical imbalance in these serious-AE terms. "No renal dose adjustment required" (a pharmacokinetic labeling statement) must never be conflated with "proven kidney efficacy" in any population, including dialysis (see below). Locator: `FLOW-SUPPLEMENT-2024`, Table S4, “Serious adverse events,” MedDRA preferred-term rows; `FDA-OZEMPIC-USPI-S038-2026`, Warnings and Precautions, “Acute Kidney Injury Due to Volume Depletion.”

## Diabetic retinopathy

Systematically collected diabetic-retinopathy events in FLOW: 504 events among 402 participants (22.8%) semaglutide vs 483 events among 398 participants (22.5%) placebo — **not different**. Separately, the broader "eye disorders" serious-adverse-event system organ class showed an imbalance: **53 (3.0%) vs 30 (1.7%)**. **This 53/30 figure is not adjudicated retinopathy complications** and cannot be assumed to be a serious subset of the 22.8%/22.5% systematically collected retinopathy rate — it must not be relabeled as a persistence or confirmation of the SUSTAIN-6 retinopathy signal (HR 1.76, 1.11–2.78; see `05`).

The current U.S. label (`FDA-OZEMPIC-USPI-S038-2026`, [DailyMed §5.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=adec4fd2-6858-4c99-91d4-531f5f2a2d79)) separately states that **rapid improvement in glucose control has been associated with temporary worsening of diabetic retinopathy** and calls for monitoring in patients with a retinopathy history. This is an early-worsening/rapid-glycemic-change warning, not proof that semaglutide is directly toxic to the retina, and it does not show that every patient with a large HbA1c reduction will worsen. Clinically, distinguish three questions: baseline retinopathy risk, anticipated speed/magnitude of HbA1c improvement, and what was actually counted in each trial. Monitoring of high-risk eyes during rapid glycemic improvement is justified by the label and SUSTAIN-6 signal; FLOW's similar systematically collected rates are reassuring within its population but do not erase the prior signal or validate the broader 53/30 SOC category as retinopathy.

Locator: systematic retinopathy counts in `FLOW-PRIMARY-2024`, Table 3, journal p.120 (local PDF p.12), and `FLOW-SUPPLEMENT-2024`, Table S4, supplement pp.28–31; serious eye-disorder SOC in the same Table S4; SUSTAIN-6 endpoint design and HR in `SUSTAIN6-RETINOPATHY-2018`, Methods §2.1.4/Results (PMCID PMC5888154).

## Hypoglycemia

61.4% of the FLOW population was on insulin at baseline. **Participants with at least one severe hypoglycemic episode: 37 (2.1%) per arm** (identical). The supportive endpoint separately counted the total number of severe hypoglycemic *episodes*: 47 vs 46. These are different counting units (participants versus episodes), not different denominators, and must be kept distinct. Especially with insulin or sulfonylurea and in advanced CKD, background hypoglycemia risk is not eliminated by this balanced trial-level result — individualized dose reduction of insulin/sulfonylurea remains standard practice when initiating semaglutide.

Locator: baseline insulin in `FLOW-PRIMARY-2024`, Table 1, journal p.113; participants with events in Table 3, journal p.120 (local PDF p.12); episode count and value ratio in Table 2, journal p.116.

## Gallbladder disease and pancreatitis

Acute gallbladder disease: 1.8% vs 2.2% (numerically lower with semaglutide). Acute pancreatitis: 10 (0.6%) vs 7 (0.4%) — numerically higher, rare in both arms, not flagged as statistically significant.

Locator: `FLOW-PRIMARY-2024`, Table 3, journal p.120 (local PDF p.12); `FLOW-SUPPLEMENT-2024`, Table S4, supplement pp.28–31.

## Gastroparesis

Current US labeling states semaglutide is not recommended in severe gastroparesis and lists postmarketing reports of ileus, intestinal obstruction, and severe constipation/fecal impaction. FLOW did not specifically enroll or report on a gastroparesis subgroup; advanced-CKD patients with baseline GI dysmotility, poor intake, or frailty warrant careful individualized monitoring.

## Malnutrition / sarcopenia

**No lean-mass, sarcopenia, or nutritional-status outcome was identified in any FLOW-related source reviewed in this project** — a genuine evidence gap, not merely an extrapolation problem. FLOW's own net weight loss (−4.10 kg vs. placebo) and its cystatin-C/creatinine concordance argument (`03`, `09`) address the creatinine-artifact question, not the separate question of whether meaningful lean-mass loss occurs, particularly relevant in elderly CKD, G4/G5 CKD, or dialysis populations. Weight locator: `FLOW-PRIMARY-2024`, Results—“Other Outcomes,” journal p.116; `FLOW-SUPPLEMENT-2024`, Figure S2B, supplement p.19. This is flagged explicitly in `12_EVIDENCE_GAPS_AND_CONTROVERSIES.md`.

## Dialysis

FLOW structurally excluded current or recent (≤90 days) dialysis and prior/awaiting solid-organ transplant recipients — there is **no FLOW randomized efficacy evidence for people already receiving maintenance dialysis**. The relevant source in this evidence set is `FLOW-DIALYSIS-SAFETY-2026` (Klein, *Diabetes Care* 2026; DOI [10.2337/dc26-0112](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-0112)), a **post hoc pooled (SUSTAIN-6+SELECT+FLOW+SOUL) individual-level analysis** of 307 people who initiated dialysis among a **source-reported pooled analysis denominator of 34,064**; only 165 remained on assigned trial drug after dialysis initiation (71 semaglutide, 94 placebo). The four parent trials' nominal randomized sample sizes sum to 34,084, leaving an unexplained 20-person difference that is preserved rather than silently harmonized. Conditioning on surviving to and initiating dialysis, then remaining on treatment, breaks the original randomized comparison for a causal dialysis-efficacy question. Locators: FLOW exclusion in `FLOW-SUPPLEMENT-2024`, “Eligibility Criteria—Exclusion,” supplement p.13 (local lines 411–442); pooled-analysis design and all source-reported denominators in `FLOW-DIALYSIS-SAFETY-2026`, PubMed structured abstract **Research Design and Methods** and **Results** (PMID 41893299). The 34,084 arithmetic check uses the randomized-N values in the structured-abstract Results of `SUSTAIN6-2016`, `SELECT-PRIMARY-2023`, `FLOW-PRIMARY-2024`, and `SOUL-PRIMARY-2025`; it is a project calculation, not a source-reported pooled denominator.

| Domain in maintenance dialysis | What this evidence set actually supports | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Continuation safety | Among the selected 165 who remained on treatment, serious adverse events were 45% vs 57% and permanent discontinuation 8.5% vs 10.6%; this offers preliminary reassurance about **continuation after dialysis starts**, not routine initiation in an established dialysis population. | Suggestive/descriptive; post hoc and selected |
| Glycemic efficacy | The pooled dialysis analysis did not test HbA1c lowering as an efficacy endpoint. Small studies may exist outside this curated set, but no verified semaglutide maintenance-dialysis glycemic RCT is available here. | **Not established in this review** |
| Weight efficacy/nutrition | It did not randomize dialysis patients for weight loss or quantify a causal post-dialysis weight effect; the semaglutide group was already lighter at dialysis initiation. In a population vulnerable to protein-energy wasting/sarcopenia, lower weight is not automatically a benefit. | **Not established; nutrition safety remains a gap** |
| Cardiovascular outcomes/mortality | MACE rates were numerically 9.7 vs 16.1 and all-cause mortality 13.8 vs 18.1 events/100 person-years after dialysis initiation. These exploratory rates are affected by small numbers, postrandomization selection, and survivor bias; the authors call for a dialysis-randomized efficacy trial. | Hypothesis-generating, **not causal efficacy proof** |
| Kidney efficacy | Once maintenance dialysis is established, prevention of CKD progression/kidney failure is no longer the same therapeutic target; no trial here establishes recovery, dialysis independence, or transplant benefit. | **No demonstrated outcome efficacy** |

Locator for every quantitative dialysis-continuation value in the table: `FLOW-DIALYSIS-SAFETY-2026`, PubMed structured abstract **Results** (PMID 41893299): 32/71 and 54/94 serious AEs, 8.5%/10.6% discontinuation, and the MACE and mortality event rates. The structured abstract **Conclusions** limits the inference to preliminary continuation safety and calls for efficacy testing.

Thus, "no renal dose adjustment required" is a pharmacokinetic statement, not evidence of glycemic, weight, kidney, or cardiovascular outcome efficacy in dialysis. Likewise, the lower post-dialysis MACE/mortality rates cannot be converted into a treatment-effect HR. Any guideline recommendation permitting initiation/continuation on dialysis (`11`) must be presented as a lower-certainty guideline judgment alongside—not instead of—this evidentiary boundary.
