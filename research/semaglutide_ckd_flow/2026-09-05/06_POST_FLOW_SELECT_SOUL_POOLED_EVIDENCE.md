# 06. Post-FLOW Evidence: SELECT, SOUL, and the Pooled Analysis

## SELECT — kidney outcomes without diabetes (`SELECT-KIDNEY-2024`, Colhoun, Nat Med 2024; parent trial `SELECT-PRIMARY-2023`)

N=17,604 (8,803/8,801); overweight/obesity + established ASCVD, **without diagnosed diabetes** (HbA1c <6.5% at entry). Semaglutide **2.4 mg SC weekly** (the SELECT/Wegovy dose, not FLOW's 1.0 mg). Prespecified secondary kidney composite (kidney-disease death, chronic KRT initiation, persistent eGFR<15, persistent ≥50% eGFR decline, or **onset of persistent macroalbuminuria**): HR **0.78 (0.63–0.96)**, P=0.02; 1.8% vs 2.2% in-trial.

**Prespecified continuous kidney outcomes:** at week 104, eGFR changed by −0.86 vs −1.61 mL/min/1.73m², an estimated between-arm difference of **+0.75 (0.43–1.06), P<0.001**; in the baseline-eGFR<60 subgroup the corresponding changes were +5.28 vs +3.09, difference **+2.19 (1.00–3.38), P<0.001**. Total eGFR slopes were −0.78 vs −1.17 mL/min/1.73m²/year, difference **+0.39 (0.30–0.48), P<0.001**; chronic slopes from week 20 onward were −0.98 vs −1.28, difference **+0.29 (0.18–0.40), P<0.001**. In the European subset with extra early measurements, the annualized acute slope from baseline through week 16 was more negative with semaglutide (−2.41 vs −1.08), difference **−1.33 (−2.68 to 0.02), exact P=0.0535** (Table display rounds to .05; not statistically significant). Week-104 UACR relative treatment difference was **−10.7% (−13.2 to −8.2), P<0.001**; by baseline UACR it was −8.1% (−10.6 to −5.6) for <30, −27.2% (−35.3 to −18.1) for 30–<300, and −31.4% (−54.9 to 4.3) for ≥300 mg/g. These are secondary continuous/surrogate outcomes with unadjusted P values; the ≥300-mg/g UACR CI crosses the null.

**What this can support:** a hypothesis-generating-to-suggestive signal that a semaglutide kidney effect is not strictly conditional on a T2D diagnosis.

**What this cannot support (five explicit limits):**
1. "Without diabetes" is not "without dysglycemia" — mean baseline HbA1c was 5.78%, but **66.4% were prediabetic** (HbA1c 5.7–6.4%) and only 33.5% normoglycemic; residual glucose-related pathophysiology is not excluded. These glycaemia-distribution figures come from `SELECT-GLYCEMIA-2024`, primary prespecified analysis structured abstract/Results (DOI 10.2337/dc24-0491; PMID 38907683), rather than from the kidney paper alone.
2. The composite is driven by its softest component: the authors' own words state the effect "was driven by the treatment effect on onset of macroalbuminuria and persistent ≥50% reduction in eGFR, with the other components being sparse," and composites excluding macroalbuminuria were not significant, owing to low event counts.
3. Mediation is fragile and was performed on a surrogate, not the hard composite: an estimated 81% (95% CI 41–120%, crossing above 100%) of the **week-104 eGFR change** was attributable to weight change. The model used baseline and week-104 values only, with no intermediate observations; it was not mediation of eGFR slope. The authors state event counts were too low to support formal mediation of the hard composite itself and call the estimate "suggestive, not definitive."
4. The eGFR<60 subgroup's numerically improved eGFR in both arms may partly reflect regression to the mean (authors' own caveat), so the +2.19 between-arm contrast must not be interpreted as literal recovery of kidney function in both groups or as a hard-outcome effect.
5. SELECT is not a dedicated CKD trial — only "just over one-fifth" of the cohort had eGFR<60 or UACR≥30 (combined denominator, exact split not reported), limiting subgroup power.

**Calibrated conclusion:** SELECT's kidney data are suggestive that a renal effect may extend beyond a strictly T2D-specific/purely-glycemic mechanism, and hypothesis-generating for a genuinely weight-mediated pathway (via its own wide-CI mediation estimate for week-104 eGFR change) — but do not establish glucose-independent, weight-independent, or hard-outcome kidney protection, and cannot be directly extrapolated to FLOW's dedicated, more advanced albuminuric-CKD population without labeling that extrapolation explicitly.

Locator for the SELECT kidney block: [primary PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11271413/), Abstract; Results “Effect of semaglutide on the main kidney endpoint,” “Effect ... on eGFR at 104 weeks,” “total and chronic eGFR slope,” “acute eGFR to week 20,” “Correlation and mediation between eGFR change and changes in body weight, blood pressure and glycated hemoglobin,” and the UACR subsection; Methods—“Correlation and mediation analysis”; Table 1 and Figs. 1–5 (`SELECT-KIDNEY-2024`; DOI 10.1038/s41591-024-03015-5; PMID 38796653; PMCID PMC11271413). The local Markdown's reporting-summary tail is excluded from evidence retrieval.

## SOUL — why the oral-route trial's kidney composite was not significant (`SOUL-PRIMARY-2025` NEJM 2025; `SOUL-KIDNEY-2026` Diabetes Care 2026; official record `SOUL-REGISTRY-2025`)

N=9,650 (4,825/4,825), T2D + ASCVD and/or CKD, **oral semaglutide 14 mg daily** (SOUL's own route/dose, not FLOW's SC 1.0 mg). Primary MACE HR **0.86 (0.77–0.96)**, P=0.006 (579/4,825 [12.0%] vs 668/4,825 [13.8%]).

**Kidney outcomes (prespecified confirmatory secondary, hierarchically gated behind MACE):** five-point composite (structurally identical to FLOW's: persistent ≥50% eGFR decline, persistent eGFR<15, chronic KRT initiation, or death kidney-related/CV) **403/4,825 (8.4%) vs 435/4,825 (9.0%), HR 0.91 (0.80–1.05), P=0.19 — not statistically significant**. Four-point composite excluding CV death: **112/4,825 (2.3%) vs 129/4,825 (2.7%), HR 0.86 (0.66–1.10), P=0.22**. Total eGFR-slope difference: **0.40 (0.27–0.53) mL/min/1.73m²/yr, nominal P<0.0001**; because the first confirmatory kidney composite failed, this later slope result is formally exploratory despite its small nominal P value. It was also smaller in absolute magnitude than FLOW's 1.16. Locator: `SOUL-KIDNEY-2026`, abstract/results; DOI 10.2337/dc25-1080, PMID 41380027, PMCID PMC12824789. The restricted cached parse is not used as sole evidence.

Locator index for both SOUL quantitative paragraphs: N, allocation, oral dose/route, follow-up, and MACE events/HR/P are from `SOUL-PRIMARY-2025`, PubMed structured abstract **Methods** and **Results** (DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2501006; PMID 40162642). The five- and four-point kidney composites, baseline mean eGFR, follow-up, and total eGFR slope are from `SOUL-KIDNEY-2026`, PubMed structured abstract **Research Design and Methods** and **Results** (DOI 10.2337/dc25-1080; PMID 41380027). Thus no SOUL number in this block depends solely on the restricted cached parse.

**Why FLOW succeeded on the hard composite and SOUL did not — population selection, not simply route:**

| | FLOW | SOUL |
|---|---|---|
| Mean baseline eGFR | 47.0 | 73.8 |
| UACR | Median 567.6 mg/g (enrollment criterion) | Not measured/collected at all |
| Placebo-arm kidney-composite event rate | ~7.5/100 patient-years | ~2.3/100 patient-years (~1/3 of FLOW's) |
| CV-death contribution as reported | ~35% **of components** (source's approximate phrase; not an exact first-event share) | ~71% of five-point composite events (reported event share) |
| Follow-up | 3.4 yr (median, early-stopped) | 47.5 months mean |

Locator index for every numeric/comparative cell in this table: FLOW baseline eGFR/UACR are `FLOW-PRIMARY-2024`, Table 1, journal p.112; FLOW follow-up and placebo rate are Results—“Trial Participants”/“Primary Outcomes,” journal pp.113, 115; FLOW's “approximately 35% of the components” is `FLOW-SUPPLEMENT-2024`, Table S2, supplement p.24 (local line 755). SOUL baseline eGFR and follow-up are `SOUL-KIDNEY-2026`, PubMed structured abstract **Results**; SOUL's placebo event rate, absence of UACR measurement, and approximately 71% CV-death contribution are in `SOUL-KIDNEY-2026`, Discussion, FLOW-versus-SOUL comparison paragraph. The 47.5-month follow-up is independently repeated in `SOUL-PRIMARY-2025`, PubMed structured abstract **Results**.

The FLOW “~35% of components” and SOUL “~71% of composite events” quantities are **not identically defined**, so no ratio or exact cross-trial difference should be calculated from them. SOUL's authors attribute the nonsignificant hard composite to lower baseline kidney risk and fewer events, “despite similar [or longer] trial duration,” and note the eGFR-slope signal “may not have had enough time to translate…into a reduction of hard kidney outcomes” in a population with a small annual kidney-function-loss rate. **This project does not conclude that injectable semaglutide “works” on the kidney while oral semaglutide “does not.”** Dose (1.0 mg SC vs 14 mg oral), route/bioavailability, and population risk all differ simultaneously between FLOW and SOUL. The calibrated statement is: SOUL's hard composite did not reach significance; lower baseline kidney risk and fewer hard events are plausible, author-offered explanations, while route, dose, population, and event mix cannot be causally isolated from one another. Its nominally significant but formally exploratory slope result supports a renal-function signal; it does not establish a hard-outcome benefit or a route-specific equivalence/difference.

**Testing-hierarchy consequence:** because the kidney composite (SOUL's first confirmatory secondary outcome) was nonsignificant, every subsequent SOUL secondary analysis is formally exploratory under the prespecified gate, not confirmatory.

## Prespecified pooled analysis (`SELECT-FLOW-SOUL-POOLED-2026`, Mann, Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol, online 2026-08-07)

Participant-level pooled analysis, **N=30,787** across FLOW (T2D+CKD, 1.0 mg SC), SELECT (established ASCVD without diabetes, 2.4 mg SC), and SOUL (T2D+ASCVD/CKD, 14 mg oral); mean follow-up 39.5–47.5 months across trials.

- **Primary pooled kidney composite (CV-death-inclusive):** 973 vs 1,134 events, **HR 0.84 (0.77–0.91)**.
- **Kidney-specific composite (CV-death-excluded):** 347 vs 416 events, **HR 0.80 (0.69–0.92)**.
- Safety broadly similar; serious adverse events numerically lower with semaglutide.

Endpoint-matched comparison is essential: the pooled **CV-death-inclusive** HR 0.84 should be compared with FLOW's five-point HR 0.76 and SOUL's five-point HR 0.91; the pooled **kidney-specific, CV-death-excluded** HR 0.80 should be compared with FLOW's four-point HR 0.79 and SOUL's four-point HR 0.86. Mixing these pairs would manufacture a false contrast. The pooled estimates reflect trials with markedly different baseline kidney risk, dose, and route, not evidence that all three trials “worked” at FLOW's magnitude. **Causal-inference caution (applied consistently across this project):** the authors' own interpretive sentence — that this “might not be explained only by [semaglutide's] glycaemic effects, weight-management effects, or both” — is a **cross-trial-consistency inference**, not a formal within-trial mediation analysis. No mediator variable (HbA1c change, weight change) is decomposed against outcome in the structured abstract, and the three pooled trials differ simultaneously in population, dose, *and* route. **Grade this specific interpretive claim hypothesis-generating**, while treating the pooled outcome HRs themselves (large N, prespecified, tight CIs) as established, high-confidence RCT-level evidence. Locator: `SELECT-FLOW-SOUL-POOLED-2026`, structured abstract/findings; DOI 10.1016/S2213-8587(26)00134-8, PMID 42567173. Full text/tables/appendix (per-trial-stratified numbers, heterogeneity statistics) have not been obtained; the paper was online/in press at the 2026-09-05 cutoff with no assigned volume/pages.

## Class effect across GLP-1 receptor agonists (`GLP1-CLASSMETA-BADVE-2025`, Badve, Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol 2025) — see also `12` and `13`

Meta-analysis of **10 T2D RCTs, N=67,769**; with SELECT added post hoc, **11 RCTs, N=85,373**. Its kidney composite was **CV-death-excluded**: kidney failure (KRT or persistent eGFR<15), sustained ≥50% eGFR decline or the nearest trial-equivalent threshold, or kidney-failure death. T2D-only pooled results: kidney composite HR **0.82 (0.73–0.93)**; kidney failure HR **0.84 (0.72–0.99)** — the CI upper bound barely excludes the null; MACE HR **0.87 (0.81–0.93)**; all-cause death HR **0.88 (0.83–0.93)**. With SELECT added post hoc: kidney composite HR 0.81 (0.72–0.92); MACE HR 0.86 (0.80–0.92); no heterogeneity vs. T2D-only trials (P-heterogeneity >0.05). Serious adverse events did not differ (RR **0.95 [0.90–1.01]**, I²=88.5%), whereas adverse-event discontinuation was more frequent with GLP-1RAs (RR **1.51 [1.18–1.94]**, I²=96.3%); both safety estimates have substantial heterogeneity. Locator: `GLP1-CLASSMETA-BADVE-2025`, PubMed/Europe PMC structured abstract, Methods/Findings; DOI 10.1016/S2213-8587(24)00271-7, PMID 39608381. **Per-molecule breakdown is not available in that abstract and has not been retrieved** — the pooled estimate is heavily weighted toward FLOW/semaglutide, which is the only molecule with a dedicated, prespecified kidney-outcomes trial in this meta-analysis. **Do not transfer the pooled class-level HR uniformly to every individual GLP-1 receptor agonist.**
