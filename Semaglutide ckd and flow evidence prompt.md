# Research Project: Semaglutide in Chronic Kidney Disease — From Early Renal Signals to FLOW and Contemporary Cardio-Kidney-Metabolic Therapy

## ROLE

You are the **research director and senior clinical evidence reviewer** for a comprehensive academic review of:

> **Semaglutide in Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD), with primary emphasis on the FLOW trial and its implications for contemporary management of type 2 diabetes and CKD.**

The final purpose of this project is NOT merely to summarize FLOW.

The project should determine:

1. how the renal evidence for semaglutide evolved before FLOW;

# XIII. SEMAGLUTIDE + SGLT2i

Answer the practical question:

> In a patient already receiving an ACEi/ARB and SGLT2 inhibitor, what is the evidence that adding semaglutide further prevents kidney failure?

Important:

FLOW had only approximately 15–16% SGLT2i use at baseline.

The SGLT2 subgroup contained relatively few events.

Therefore distinguish:

### Supported
- no convincing evidence of harmful interaction;
- eGFR slope benefit appears directionally preserved;
- UACR improvement appears preserved;
- CV/mortality benefit does not clearly differ by SGLT2i use.

### Not established
- definitive randomized additive hard-kidney benefit;
- exact incremental absolute risk reduction;
- superiority of combination over either monotherapy.

Search supporting evidence from:
- AMPLITUDE-O SGLT2i subgroup;
- SGLT2 outcome trials with background GLP-1 RA;
- randomized surrogate-endpoint combination trials;

Every major clinical statement in the final manuscript should be traceable.

Examples:

CLAIM:
“Semaglutide reduces major kidney outcomes in albuminuric CKD with T2D.”

SOURCE:
FLOW primary RCT.

EVIDENCE LEVEL:
High.

---

CLAIM:
“Semaglutide provides additional hard kidney protection when added to an SGLT2 inhibitor.”

SOURCE:
FLOW SGLT2 subgroup.

EVIDENCE LEVEL:
Insufficient for definitive claim.

Preferred wording:
“Available subgroup evidence does not show meaningful heterogeneity by SGLT2i use, but the subgroup was underpowered to establish additive hard kidney benefit.”

---

CLAIM:
“Semaglutide plus finerenone has proven outcome benefit.”

EVIDENCE:
Not established.

Do not make this claim.

# XXIX. REQUIRED FINAL CLINICAL SYNTHESIS

The conclusion must avoid both extremes:

Do not say:

“FLOW proves semaglutide should replace SGLT2 inhibitors.”

Also do not say:

“Semaglutide only improves albuminuria.”

Instead evaluate whether the evidence supports a conclusion approximately along these lines:

> FLOW changed semaglutide from a glucose-, weight-, and cardiovascular-focused therapy with suggestive renal signals into a therapy with randomized evidence for clinically important kidney protection in patients with T2D and albuminuric CKD.

Then qualify:

> SGLT2 inhibitors remain foundational kidney-protective therapy because of their extensive dedicated CKD evidence base, while semaglutide offers a complementary therapeutic profile incorporating kidney, ASCVD, mortality, glycemic, and weight benefits.

And:

> Evidence supporting combination therapy is biologically and clinically compelling but substantially stronger for complementary benefit in general than for a formally proven additive reduction in hard kidney outcomes from specific semaglutide + SGLT2i or semaglutide + finerenone combinations.

Verify every part against the evidence before adopting this wording.
